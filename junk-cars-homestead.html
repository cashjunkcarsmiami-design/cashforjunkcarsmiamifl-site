Selling a car that has been parked for a long time

Separate current facts from memories

State when the car last ran if you know, and identify older photos or repair records by date. Mention missing components, flat tires and visible damage. You do not need to start the vehicle to make an initial inquiry.

Describe the ground and entrance

Tell us whether the parking surface is paved, grass or another material. Explain gates, nearby equipment and obstacles. Share photos from an accessible position; do not lift the vehicle or clear heavy obstacles for a picture.

Confirm the property contact

Identify who can provide entry and where the keys and ownership records are kept. If objects belonging to someone else surround the vehicle, coordinate with their owner. Confirm access before agreeing to a pickup appointment.

What to send for an offer

  • Year, make and model, plus any known missing components.
  • Whether it starts and rolls; keys and ownership document availability.
  • Current photos of accessible areas and the pickup city or ZIP code.
  • For collection: the exact location, access restrictions and handoff contact.

A description lets us review your vehicle. Confirm the purchase amount, payment arrangement, required documents and any collection costs before you accept. If you do not have the title, explain which records are available first; not every situation can be accepted.

Can I use old photos of a stored car?

Old photos can help identify it if you label them as old. They do not confirm its current condition. Add recent pictures when practical, explain the areas you cannot inspect and ask what other information is needed for an offer.
